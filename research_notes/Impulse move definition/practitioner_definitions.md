# Practitioner definitions of an impulse (impulsive / displacement) price move

Reading key for all sections:
- Claim tags: **[RULE]** = binary test stated by the school. **[GUIDELINE]** = tendency the school itself treats as non-binding. **[OPINION]** = practitioner or vendor judgement. **[EVIDENCE]** = independent empirical test.
- Source grades: **A** = author or school's own site or glossary. **B** = reputable reference or summary. **C** = vendor, educator or forum content, including named Brooks forum members who are not Brooks. **D** = anonymous or unattributed.

Source-access limits that apply throughout:
- I did not read the primary books: Frost & Prechter, Brooks's trilogy, Wyckoff's course or Huddleston's mentorship. O'Reilly's Brooks and Elliott pages returned HTTP 403.
- Page content came through a summarising fetch tool, so "quotes" below should be checked against the live page before they are reproduced as verbatim.

## Elliott Wave: hard rules vs guidelines of a five-wave impulse

### Takeaway
Elliott Wave is the only school here with a compact set of binary rules, and there are three of them. They constrain the geometry of a labelled count, with no minimum size, duration or volume, so they do not on their own tell you whether a move is "impulsive" in the everyday sense. Fibonacci ratios and volume behaviour are tendencies, and the one independent test found did not support the Fibonacci ratio claims.

### Cited Findings
**Rules (objective once a count is labelled; numeric only as the 100% limit and relative lengths)**
- [RULE] Wave 2 cannot retrace past the start of wave 1. — [Wikipedia, Elliott wave principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliott_wave_principle) (B)
- [RULE] Wave 3 cannot be the shortest of waves 1, 3 and 5. It need not be the longest. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliott_wave_principle) (B); the "need not be longest" wording is from [WaveBasis, core rules](https://wavebasis.com/docs/frequently-asked-questions/automatic-wave-counts/what-are-the-core-elliott-wave-rules/) (C), seen only as a search snippet
- [RULE] Wave 4 cannot enter the price territory of wave 1. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliott_wave_principle) (B)
- [RULE, exception] Overlap of waves 4 and 1 is the marker of diagonals, which occur in ending diagonals and normally in leading diagonals. — [Nasdaq article on motive-wave variations](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/ellliott-wave-basics-variations-motive-waves-2011-06-28), search snippet only because the full fetch timed out; author not confirmed (C)

**Guidelines (non-binding; some numeric)**
- [GUIDELINE] Alternation: waves 2 and 4 tend to differ in form, so a sharp wave 2 suggests a sideways wave 4 and vice versa. — [StockCharts ChartSchool](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/market-analysis/elliott-wave-analysis-articles/guidelines-for-applying-elliott-wave-theory) (B; no named author, recommends Frost & Prechter as the main primer)
- [GUIDELINE] Equality: two motive sub-waves tend toward equality, and when wave 3 extends, wave 5 is roughly equal to wave 1. — [StockCharts](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/market-analysis/elliott-wave-analysis-articles/guidelines-for-applying-elliott-wave-theory) (B)
- [GUIDELINE] Corrections often end in the territory of the prior wave 4 of lesser degree. Channeling is used to project wave ends. — [StockCharts](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/market-analysis/elliott-wave-analysis-articles/guidelines-for-applying-elliott-wave-theory) (B)
- [GUIDELINE, numeric] Wave 2 often retraces no more than about 61.8% of wave 1. Wave 4 typically retraces less than 38.2% of wave 3. Wave 3 frequently extends wave 1 by about 1.618 times. — [Wikipedia, citing Frost & Prechter for the 2 and 4 figures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliott_wave_principle) (B)
- [GUIDELINE, volume] Third waves "usually generate the greatest volume and price movement" and are most often the extended wave. Volume in the fifth is usually less than in the third, except in extended fifths. EWI says these are "stated not as rules, but as guidelines." — [Elliott Wave International, Wave Personality](https://www.elliottwave.com/?p=8491) (A)
- [GUIDELINE, numeric but not a threshold] "About half" of first waves are part of the basing process. — [EWI](https://www.elliottwave.com/?p=8491) (A)
- [OPINION, EWI's use of personality] Several counts can be admissible under all known rules, and wave personality is the tool for choosing among them. — [EWI](https://www.elliottwave.com/?p=8491) (A)

**Evidence and critique**
- [EVIDENCE] Batchelor & Ramyar (2005) found no significant difference between the frequency of price and time ratios in Dow Jones cycles and what random series produce. — reported by [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliott_wave_principle) (B; paper not read)
- [OPINION, academic or practitioner critics] Aronson (2006) called the popular practice "not a legitimate theory, but a story," arguing that loose rules and nested waves can fit any history. Mandelbrot & Hudson (2004) called the record "at best mixed" and said subjective judgement matters more than replicable verdicts. Neely (1990) argued strict orthodox application skews accuracy. — all via [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliott_wave_principle) (B)

### Inferences
- The rules are falsifiers of a proposed count and do not detect impulses. Many non-impulsive paths can satisfy them, and the same price path can admit several valid counts, which EWI itself concedes.
- The rules test relationships among wave endpoints, so results depend on where the analyst places those endpoints and at what degree. Making them into a detector needs an added swing-definition rule that the school does not supply in the sources read.

### Gaps
- Frost & Prechter's exact wording and page references were not verified from the book. The three-rule list is well corroborated through secondary sources.
- Search snippets mentioned tolerating 10-15% overlap in futures and looser rules for leveraged securities. I could not trace these to a named authority. Treat them as unsupported (D) and as contrary to the orthodox rule.
- Only one empirical study was reached, and it addresses Fibonacci ratios, not the three rules. I found no systematic out-of-sample test of the rules.

## Wyckoff: sign of strength / weakness and effort vs result

### Takeaway
Wyckoff definitions are objective in direction but relative and judgemental in magnitude: "increasing spread and relatively higher volume" has no multiplier. Numbers in the school's materials concern trade selection and point-and-figure counting, not what qualifies as an SOS or SOW.

### Cited Findings
**Definitions (conditions stated, but qualitative)**
- [RULE-LIKE, qualitative] Sign of Strength: "a price advance on increasing spread and relatively higher volume." — [Wyckoff Analytics, The Wyckoff Method](https://www.wyckoffanalytics.com/wyckoff-method/) (A, a leading modern Wyckoff teaching site, though not Wyckoff himself)
- [RULE-LIKE, qualitative] Sign of Weakness: "a down-move to (or slightly past) the lower boundary of the TR," typically with more spread and volume on the downside. The definition is location-dependent on a trading range. — [Wyckoff Analytics](https://www.wyckoffanalytics.com/wyckoff-method/) (A)
- [INTERPRETIVE PRINCIPLE] Law of Effort vs Result "provides an early warning of a possible change in trend": divergence between volume and price can signal a coming reversal. — [Wyckoff Analytics](https://www.wyckoffanalytics.com/wyckoff-method/) (A)
- [CONTEXT] Phase D shows "consistent dominance of demand over supply"; in Phase E price leaves the range and "demand is in full control." The distribution side mirrors this. — [Wyckoff Analytics](https://www.wyckoffanalytics.com/wyckoff-method/) (A)

**Numbers present, but not impulse thresholds**
- Reward at least three times the risk (buying and selling tests); P&F box sizes and 3-box reversals; "three-quarters or more" of issues moving with the market; distribution weakness confirmed by a decline below the trading-range midpoint. — [Wyckoff Analytics](https://www.wyckoffanalytics.com/wyckoff-method/) (A)

**Secondary glosses**
- [OPINION] Volume should be judged "against its own recent baseline rather than in absolute terms." The page gives no multiples or cutoffs and links the same idea to Tom Williams' Volume Spread Analysis. — [LuxAlgo, Effort vs Result](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/effort-vs-result/) (C, indicator vendor)
- [OPINION] Divergence warns of opposition but can resolve either way, so practitioners wait for confirmation; a narrow-spread, shrinking-volume range break is treated as a possible trap; spot forex has only tick volume. — returned for the same search as [LuxAlgo](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/effort-vs-result/) and similar pages; attribution to a specific page not confirmed (C/D)

**Evidence**
- None of the sources read offers an empirical test of SOS, SOW or effort vs result.

### Inferences
- An SOS is the nearest Wyckoff analogue of an impulse, and it requires both price and volume expansion, unlike the other three schools' core definitions.
- The word "relatively" means any implementation must pick its own baseline and multiple. That choice belongs to the implementer and not to the school.

### Gaps
- Wyckoff's original course wording was not consulted, so SOS and SOW definitions here reflect the modern Wyckoff Analytics formulation.
- I found no reliable empirical study of these definitions in this search, and I did not run a dedicated academic search.

## ICT / Smart Money Concepts: displacement, FVGs and BOS

### Takeaway
I found no canonical, citable numeric definition. Secondary sources converge on a description ("aggressive," one-directional, large-bodied candles, usually leaving an FVG), and one explicitly says no fixed threshold exists. Huddleston's own wording was not obtained, so "no canonical definition" here means none in accessible sources, not a verified statement about his materials.

### Cited Findings
- [OPINION/description] "Displacement is an aggressive move with full-bodied candles"; "the easiest way to spot it is just looking for fair value gaps." — [SozAI transcript of "Market Structure Shift - ICT Concepts"](https://sozai.app/transcript/market-structure-shift-ict-concepts/) (C/D, unnamed presenter, not established to be Huddleston)
- [OPINION/description] "A fast, one-sided move: a run of large-bodied candles closing in the same direction." — [LuxAlgo, Displacement](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/displacement/) (C, vendor)
- [OPINION] "There is no fixed threshold"; "reasonable traders disagree at the margin." Bodies are compared with the "last few dozen bars," and "three to five strong closes" is only an illustration. — [LuxAlgo](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/displacement/) (C)
- [OPINION/description] A run of candles in one direction, usually with small wicks and large bodies. The article gives no measurable body, wick or timeframe criteria, cites no studies and offers no verified results. — [Tradervue, The ICT Trading Strategy](https://www.tradervue.com/blog/the-ict-trading-strategy) (C, journal vendor; no primary-source citations)

**Role of FVG**
- [OPINION] An FVG is the "residue" of a fast leg and "strong supporting evidence rather than a strict requirement." — [LuxAlgo](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/displacement/) (C)
- [CONFLICT in emphasis] The SozAI presenter treats FVGs as the easiest identifier of displacement, while LuxAlgo denies that they are required. — [SozAI](https://sozai.app/transcript/market-structure-shift-ict-concepts/); [LuxAlgo](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/displacement/)
- [OPINION] Displacement occurs just after a liquidity level is breached and often creates both an FVG and an MSS. — search snippet from an ICT-concepts explainer, page not identified among [these results](https://trendspider.com/blog/ict-basics-a-beginners-guide/) (D until verified)

**Role of BOS / MSS**
- [OPINION] A break of structure is a level event, while displacement describes the character of the move. A BOS or change of character is "only acted on when the breaking leg shows displacement." — [LuxAlgo](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/displacement/) (C)
- [OPINION/description] Look for "displacement below a previous low or if it is bullish above a previous high," preferably after price engaged a higher-timeframe level. No numeric criteria. — [SozAI](https://sozai.app/transcript/market-structure-shift-ict-concepts/) (C/D)
- [OPINION] MSS is a possible reversal, initially a lower high in an uptrend or a higher low in a downtrend, followed by displacement through a key level. — [TradingView idea, MSS in ICT trading](https://pl.tradingview.com/chart/GBPUSD/WHnHfxfR-Market-Structure-Shift-Meaning-and-Use-in-ICT-Trading) (C/D, user-generated; search snippet)

**Evidence**
- [EVIDENCE: none found] The only reviewed article addressing outcomes gives a qualitative verdict that the strategy works "for some but not all traders," with no data. — [Tradervue](https://www.tradervue.com/blog/the-ict-trading-strategy) (C). This is not evidence of efficacy.

### Inferences
- The FVG is the only objective element commonly linked to displacement, assuming the standard three-candle formulation, which I did not verify in this search. It is a by-product marker and is not agreed to be necessary.
- As taught in these sources, the definition is comparative and partly circular: a BOS counts when it has displacement, and displacement is often spotted by the FVG it leaves.
- Any numeric ICT displacement filter (ATR or body-percentage cutoffs) should be treated as an implementer's choice unless traced to Huddleston.

### Gaps
- Huddleston's own definition was not retrieved. A targeted search failed, and his mentorship videos were not available as verified transcripts.
- I found no independent empirical test of displacement or ICT performance. I did not run a dedicated academic search.

## Al Brooks: spikes, breakout bars and always-in moves

### Takeaway
Brooks defines the terms in his own glossary, mostly qualitatively, with approximate numbers only in places ("one to about five bars"). "Always in" is a directional judgement, not a formula. The bar counts and probabilities commonly attributed to him were found here only as forum reports and need checking against his books.

### Cited Findings
**Brooks's own glossary (A; several entries were truncated by the fetch tool)**
- [DEFINITION, objective] Breakout: the current bar's high or low extends beyond some prior price of significance, such as a swing high or low. — [Brooks glossary](https://www.brookstradingcourse.com/price-action-trading-terms-glossary/)
- [DEFINITION, partly qualitative] Breakout bar: "A bar that creates a breakout. It is usually a strong trend bar." — [Brooks glossary](https://www.brookstradingcourse.com/price-action-trading-terms-glossary/)
- [DEFINITION, objective] Trend bar: a bar with a body, meaning the close was above or below the open. As fetched, the entry sets no minimum body size, but the entry was truncated. — [Brooks glossary](https://www.brookstradingcourse.com/price-action-trading-terms-glossary/)
- [DEFINITION, numeric but approximate] Breakout pullback: "A small pullback of one to about five bars that occurs within a few bars after a breakout." — [Brooks glossary](https://www.brookstradingcourse.com/price-action-trading-terms-glossary/)
- [DEFINITION] Follow-through: "one or more bars that extend the move" after the initial move. — [Brooks glossary](https://www.brookstradingcourse.com/price-action-trading-terms-glossary/)
- [DEFINITION, qualitative] Spike and channel: a breakout into a trend in which the follow-through is a channel with less momentum. — [Brooks glossary](https://www.brookstradingcourse.com/price-action-trading-terms-glossary/)
- [DEFINITION, qualitative] Climax: "A move that has gone too far too fast" and has reversed to a range or opposite trend. — [Brooks glossary](https://www.brookstradingcourse.com/price-action-trading-terms-glossary/)
- [DEFINITION, approximate] Measured move: a leg "about equal in size to something earlier in the trend." — [Brooks glossary](https://www.brookstradingcourse.com/price-action-trading-terms-glossary/)
- [DEFINITION, conceptual] Always in: "If you have to be in the market at all times, either long or short, this is whatever your current position is" (truncated). — [Brooks glossary](https://www.brookstradingcourse.com/price-action-trading-terms-glossary/)
- [NOTE] As returned, the glossary has no standalone "spike" or "strong breakout" entry. — [Brooks glossary](https://www.brookstradingcourse.com/price-action-trading-terms-glossary/)

**Book text, seen through search snippets only (page returned 403)**
- [DEFINITION, qualitative] A spike is one or more trend bars signalling the market is breaking into a clear always-in situation, effectively a breakaway gap. It usually forms in the first hour, often within the first few bars of the day. The following pullback can be as short as one bar or as long as a couple of dozen bars. — [Brooks, *Trading Price Action Trends*, ch. 21, O'Reilly](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/trading-price-action/9781118066515/OEBPS/9781118166253_epub_c_21.htm) (A via snippet; verify)

**Second-hand numbers (forum, not Brooks's own words)**
- [OPINION/REPORT] A member says Al "normally refers to 3-5 consecutive bear bars as being enough to switch to AIS." A moderator adds, "Depending on the size of the bear bars you need more of them to switch." Another says some flips happen "after just 1 or 2 bars." — [Brooks forum thread](https://www.brookstradingcourse.com/es/support-forum/general-trading-discussion/qts-on-always-in-direction-flipping/) (C)
- [OPINION/REPORT] A member attributes to Al a 60% chance of a second leg after a climax up move, and calls a reversal "within 5 bars" a 75% event. — [same thread](https://www.brookstradingcourse.com/es/support-forum/general-trading-discussion/qts-on-always-in-direction-flipping/) (C). These are unverified, and even if accurately reported they would be discretionary estimates. No backtest was shown, so they are not evidence.
- [OPINION] "3-5+ consecutive strong trend bars against you" can temporarily reverse always-in, especially in weak trends. A poster's "1 Big, 2 Medium, 3+ Small" breakout formula is offered as a claim, and the thread asks for it to be verified. — [Always in confusion thread](https://www.brookstradingcourse.com/support-forum/13-always-in/always-in-confusion/) (C)
- [REPORTED QUOTE] "Always in is a swing concept that applies to trends," quoted by a forum poster from Brooks's *Reversals*, ch. 15. — [Always in confusion thread](https://www.brookstradingcourse.com/support-forum/13-always-in/always-in-confusion/) (C reporting A; verify against the book)

**Evidence**
- None of the Brooks sources read includes a statistical study supporting these bar counts or probabilities.

### Inferences
- Brooks's system offers the most explicit bar-level vocabulary, but its core strength judgement ("strong," "clear") remains discretionary. The bar-count rule itself depends on bar size, per the moderator's comment.
- Treat "3-5 bars" as a teaching heuristic until confirmed from the books, not as a Brooks threshold.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve the books or full glossary entries (truncation; O'Reilly 403). His strong-breakout criteria, stated probabilities and any explicit numeric always-in conditions remain unverified.
- Forum search snippets also described always-in as a bias where probability is above 50%, but I did not confirm which thread said this or whether Brooks did.
- No "strong breakout" definition was found in his glossary, which may reflect the fetch rather than the source.

## Minimum size, candle count or volume thresholds across schools

### Takeaway
In the sources reviewed, no school states an absolute minimum size, a required candle count or a numeric volume threshold for a move to qualify as impulsive. The numbers that do exist are structural (Elliott), approximate descriptors or forum heuristics (Brooks), or unrelated to the definition (Wyckoff).

### Cited Findings
| School | Minimum size | Candle count | Volume threshold | Numbers that do exist |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Elliott | None found; only relative tests. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliott_wave_principle) | Five waves in structure, not candles. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliott_wave_principle) | None; volume is a guideline. [EWI](https://www.elliottwave.com/?p=8491) | 100% wave 2 limit (rule); 61.8%, 38.2%, 1.618 (guidelines). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliott_wave_principle) |
| Wyckoff | None; "increasing spread." [Wyckoff Analytics](https://www.wyckoffanalytics.com/wyckoff-method/) | None | Relative only: "relatively higher volume." [Wyckoff Analytics](https://www.wyckoffanalytics.com/wyckoff-method/) | 3:1 reward/risk, P&F boxes, range midpoint: not impulse criteria. [Wyckoff Analytics](https://www.wyckoffanalytics.com/wyckoff-method/) |
| ICT/SMC | None; "no fixed threshold." [LuxAlgo](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/displacement/) (C) | None; "three to five strong closes" is illustrative. [LuxAlgo](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/displacement/) (C) | None found | None in the [SozAI transcript](https://sozai.app/transcript/market-structure-shift-ict-concepts/) beyond time-of-day references |

[Glossary](https://www.brookstradingcourse.com/price-action-trading-terms-glossary/) |

### Inferences
- Across schools, the shared idea is relative expansion: larger or faster moves compared with recent context. Only Wyckoff makes volume central to the definition.
- Any numeric impulse detector (ATR multiple, body ratio, volume z-score) is a researcher's operationalisation and should not be presented as these schools' own rule.
- The practitioner material supplies definitions, not validation. The only independent test reached here (Batchelor & Ramyar) was unfavourable to Elliott Fibonacci ratios, and vendor or forum statements should not be counted as evidence.

### Gaps
- Absence claims are limited to the sources read. The primary books, especially Brooks's trilogy and Huddleston's videos, could contain thresholds not seen here.
- The academic literature was not searched in depth, as this task focused on practitioner definitions.
