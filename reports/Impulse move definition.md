# No School Validates the Six-Check Impulse Filter

**In the sources reached, no school defines an impulse with a numeric minimum for size, candle count or volume, and no tested evidence supports any of the script's six thresholds.** Elliott Wave has the only binary rules, but they check a labelled five-wave count and do not detect impulses. Wyckoff requires price and volume expansion in relative terms only, while ICT/SMC and Al Brooks describe strong moves mostly in discretionary language. Quantitative research offers formulas and jump tests, but I found no study that conditions on a large 4H or 15m move in crypto perpetuals and measures what follows. The script is therefore a reasonable operationalisation of ideas drawn from these schools, not an implementation of any school's rule. I also found no test of low volume nodes (LVNs) inside impulse legs, so that part of the setup remains opinion. Your own backtests on BTC, ETH, SOL and XRP found no edge for this setup, which is context only and not part of the evidence weighed here. The main limit is access: Frost & Prechter, Brooks's books, Wyckoff's course, Huddleston's mentorship, and Dalton's and Steidlmayer's books were not read, so "none found" never means "proven absent."

## Only Elliott has binary rules, and they test a count

The schools answer different questions. Elliott asks whether a labelled structure is admissible, Wyckoff whether demand or supply is dominating at a location, ICT/SMC whether a level break had enough force, and Brooks whether the market has entered a clear trend state. Quantitative work asks whether a move is statistically unusual or predictive. Only the last can validate anything, and it rarely tests these definitions.

| School | Conditions for an impulse | Status | Numeric thresholds | Source limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Elliott Wave | Wave 2 stays above the start of wave 1, wave 3 is not the shortest, and wave 4 stays out of wave 1's territory ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliott_wave_principle)) | Objective rules once a count is labelled | 100% wave 2 limit. The 61.8%, 38.2% and 1.618 figures are guidelines. No size, candle or volume minimum | Secondary source for the rules; book not read |
| Wyckoff | A sign of strength is an advance on widening spread and "relatively higher volume"; a sign of weakness is tied to the trading range boundary ([Wyckoff Analytics](https://www.wyckoffanalytics.com/wyckoff-method/)) | Qualitative definition | None for the definition. The 3:1 reward/risk and point-and-figure numbers concern other decisions | Modern school wording, not Wyckoff's course |
| ICT/SMC | Displacement is a fast, one-sided run of large-bodied candles, often leaving a fair value gap (FVG), with a BOS counting when the breaking leg shows it ([LuxAlgo](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/displacement/)) | Opinion | None; the vendor says no fixed threshold exists, and three to five strong closes is only an illustration | Vendor and transcript sources; Huddleston's wording not retrieved |
| Al Brooks | A breakout exceeds a significant prior price ([glossary](https://www.brookstradingcourse.com/price-action-trading-terms-glossary/)); a spike is one or more trend bars breaking into a clear always-in state ([book snippet](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/trading-price-action/9781118066515/OEBPS/9781118166253_epub_c_21.htm)) | Breakout is objective; strength is discretionary | One to about five bars describes a breakout pullback, not an impulse. Three to five bars for an always-in flip is [forum-reported](https://www.brookstradingcourse.com/es/support-forum/general-trading-discussion/qts-on-always-in-direction-flipping/) and depends on bar size | Truncated glossary, snippet and forum only |
| Quantitative | Kaufman's efficiency ratio (ER) uses net change over summed absolute changes ([TA-Lib](https://ta-lib.org/functions/er.html)); jump tests use a normalised return against a null distribution ([Nunes & Ruas](https://repositorio.iscte-iul.pt/bitstream/10071/31168/1/article_100198.pdf)) | Formal methods; thresholds are tested only for jump detection | Jump tests have critical values. ER and ATR cut-offs are [conventions](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/kaufman-efficiency-ratio/) | Mostly abstracts and partial text |

Validation is thin across all four practitioner schools. **The one independent Elliott test found, Batchelor & Ramyar (2005), saw no significant difference between Dow ratio frequencies and random series** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliott_wave_principle), paper not read). It tests Fibonacci ratios, not the three rules. Elliott Wave International itself [concedes](https://www.elliottwave.com/?p=8491) that several counts can satisfy every rule, and it treats volume as a guideline. No Wyckoff or Brooks source read offered a statistical test, and the 60% and 75% probabilities [attributed to Brooks on his forum](https://www.brookstradingcourse.com/es/support-forum/general-trading-discussion/qts-on-always-in-direction-flipping/) are unverified estimates with no backtest shown.

The SMC evidence is the most contested. The most rigorous test found, Mahadzva's 2026 preprint on four G10 pairs, [reports no evidence the concepts work as taught](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7430998), but it was seen only as a search summary and it is unreviewed FX evidence. A [peer-reviewed gold study](https://www.informatica.si/index.php/informatica/article/view/12899/6712) reports a rules-only net return of 38.6%, yet its own ablations credit calibration and risk constraints as the primary drivers of risk-adjusted gains, and its comparison with buy-and-hold was not confirmed. "Not shown to work" is the defensible phrasing, not "shown not to work." I found no standalone test of BOS as a continuation signal.

## All six checks are implementer choices, and some partly overlap

The script's concepts have recognisable sources, but its numbers do not. The BOS check is the most defensible as a concept, since Brooks's breakout gives an objective form, though the result depends on a swing definition that none of the sources supplies in testable form. The other five are thresholds chosen by the implementer.

| Script check | Closest support | Support for the number | Assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Break of structure | Brooks breakout; SMC BOS | Not applicable | Objective once swings are defined; no standalone test found |
| Size >= 4 x ATR | The shared idea of relative expansion | None; the only ATR multiple seen is a vendor's [2 x ATR trigger](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/indicator/RqEk3iZu-atr-breakouts/), with no outcome data | Volatility scaling is sensible, but 4 is unsupported |
| 2-10 candles | Brooks's "one or more" bars; SMC's illustrative three to five | None; Brooks's description would admit the one-bar spike that this check excludes | Spans 30 minutes to 2.5 hours on 15m and 8 to 40 hours on 4H |
| Efficiency >= 0.30 | Variant of Kaufman ER | None; thresholds are conventions, and I found no peer-reviewed test of continuation after high ER | Stricter than ER because it penalises wicks |
| Volume >= 1.5 x on 15m, 1.1 x on 4H | Wyckoff's relative volume | None; no source supports either multiple or a timeframe split | Direction of the idea has mixed evidence |
| >= 70% candles in direction | SMC's same-direction closes; Brooks's trend bars | None | A count that ignores candle size |

Elliott's rules are absent from all six checks, so no check can tell whether a leg is a valid wave. A two-candle leg also cannot display five waves on that timeframe (my inference), so "impulse" here means a strong leg, not an Elliott impulse. The Wyckoff match is partial too: the script requires volume alongside size, but it has no trading-range location and no effort-versus-result divergence test.

**The checks' strictness changes sharply with candle count, because the thresholds are fixed while the random baseline moves.** The table below is my own arithmetic for a driftless market and is not sourced evidence. It assumes Gaussian, continuous price paths, ATR close to 1.6 times per-bar sigma, and rounding the 70% test up to whole candles. The ER column follows the 1/sqrt(n) baseline; the efficiency column halves it, because an expected high-low range is about twice an expected absolute return under those assumptions. Crypto's fat tails, volatility clustering and autocorrelation will move all of these values, so they orient and do not calibrate.

| Candles (n) | ER baseline | Script efficiency baseline | 4 x ATR as z-score of the leg | Random chance of passing 70% | Average candle range at which efficiency starts to bind |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 0.71 | 0.35 | 4.5 | 25% (2 of 2) | 6.7 x ATR |
| 3 | 0.58 | 0.29 | 3.7 | 12.5% (3 of 3) | 4.4 x ATR |
| 5 | 0.45 | 0.22 | 2.9 | 18.8% (4 of 5) | 2.7 x ATR |
| 10 | 0.32 | 0.16 | 2.0 | 17.2% (7 of 10) | 1.3 x ATR |

Three things follow. First, 4 x ATR is a rare event at two candles but only about a two-sigma event at ten, before counting the many overlapping windows a scanner checks. So the same "impulse" label covers very different statistical events. Second, efficiency of 0.30 is near the driftless baseline at two or three candles and about twice it at ten. Third, efficiency is largely implied by the size check on short legs: a 4 x ATR leg fails 0.30 only if its candles average more than 13.3/n times ATR, which binds mainly near ten candles. The 70% test also overlaps with size and efficiency, since a large net move tends to bring same-direction candles with it. The six checks therefore carry fewer independent tests than their number suggests, and each check's marginal rejection count can be measured directly on your candidate set.

## Volume's sign depends on context, and no intraday perpetual test exists

Requiring high volume assumes that volume confirms direction, and the tested literature does not support that as a general rule. Gervais, Kaniel & Mingelgrin found [a high-volume return premium](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:jfinan:v:56:y:2001:i:3:p:877-919) in daily NYSE data, but the premium is not conditioned on move direction. Lee & Swaminathan found [high-volume winners reverse sooner](https://www.johnhcochrane.com/s/lee_swaminathan_returns_volume_JF.pdf) over multi-year horizons. Llorente et al. found [the sign depends on informed trading](https://archive.nyu.edu/jspui/bitstream/2451/26698/1/FIN-00-032.pdf): volume leaned toward continuation in small, high-spread stocks and toward reversal in large, liquid ones. All of this is US equity evidence at far longer horizons than a 15m bar.

The closest crypto result is Bianchi, Babiak & Dickerson, who found that [volume shocks predict returns only in interaction with past returns](https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/id/eprint/172093), with reversal after low-volume intervals. **That supports "low-volume moves are more likely to reverse" better than "high-volume moves continue," and the two claims are not equivalent.** The sample is BTC-denominated coins across many exchanges, the [Sharpe of up to 2.96 is gross](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/predicting-crypto-asset-returns-with-past-returns-volume/), and returns are amplified in smaller, less liquid pairs. The script's volume gate may therefore screen out some weak moves, but nothing here shows that it selects continuations, or that 1.5 x and 1.1 x are the right cut-offs. Comparing perpetual volume with the same venue's own history at the same time of day is sound reasoning from the research notes, not a tested result.

## Crypto continuation evidence is conditional and sits at the wrong horizon

No source supports an unconditional claim that impulses continue. Time-series momentum in BTC, XRP and ETH is [reported by Liu & Tsyvinski](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:rfinst:v:34:y:2021:i:6:p:2689-2727.) at daily and weekly frequencies (per a search summary, without effect sizes), while [Huang et al.](https://profiles.wustl.edu/en/publications/time-series-momentum-is-it-there/) dispute the original time-series momentum result in other asset classes. At daily frequency, [Zaremba et al.](https://www.x-mol.com/paper/1442395529393532928) find reversal across coins, attributed to illiquidity, but momentum in the largest and most tradable ones. Intraday Bitcoin has [both momentum and reversal](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/ecofin/v62y2022ics1062940822000833.html), and those patterns change around large jumps, while [half-hour momentum](https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/100181) is about session timing, not move size.

The nearest evidence on large moves is about jumps. Scaillet et al. found that [Bitcoin jumps cause a significant, persistent price change](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1704.08175), averaging +4.7% and -4.1%, with activity and illiquidity reverting in under half an hour. A persistent level change is not further drift after the move, which is what a pullback entry needs, and the data is tick-level from Mt. Gox in 2011 to 2013. Formal jump tests also translate poorly: [Christensen et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.10925) show that lower-frequency sampling often mislabels volatility bursts as jumps (in equity and FX data). A 15m or 4H impulse is best called a large volatility-scaled move.

Costs tighten the question. Reported net-positive trend results sit at longer horizons or in weak sources: [Zarattini et al.](https://ideas.repec.org/p/chf/rpseri/rp2580.html) is a working paper seen through summaries, and [AdaptiveTrend](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11708) is a single 6-hour-bar preprint whose reported Sharpe of 2.41 over a short window warrants scepticism. [Han, Kang & Ryu](https://acfr.aut.ac.nz/__data/assets/pdf_file/0009/918729/Time_Series_and_Cross_Sectional_Momentum_in_the_Cryptocurrency_Market_with_IA.pdf) report that many statistically significant momentum portfolios earn insignificant profits after costs, and that liquidation matters, though this comes from a search summary because the full text was blocked. At a 4 bps taker fee, a round trip costs 8 bps before slippage and funding (my arithmetic), which weighs more on 15m than on 4H. Funding and liquidation effects on post-impulse returns were not quantified in any source reached. One further hazard for 15m tests is that [Bitcoin's positive returns concentrate at the turns of 15-minute candles](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10015199/).

## LVNs inside impulse legs have no tested support

**I found no peer-reviewed or transparent test of LVNs as trading levels, none of LVNs inside impulse legs, and none of volume-profile nodes as support or resistance in crypto.** What surfaced were [indicators that build the setup](https://www.tradingview.com/scripts/wave-analysis/) and chart statistics, with no method, sample, costs or baseline. Proprietary tests may exist, and the search may have missed work under terms such as "volume by price."

The claim is also poorly specified. No standard numeric definition turned up: LuxAlgo describes [valleys with no volume threshold or bin count](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/high-low-volume-nodes.md), and one TradingView script relies on [a user-set detection percentage](https://www.tradingview.com/script/yhGyDKDr-Volume-Profile-LVN/). The same LuxAlgo page allows either quick rejection or slicing straight through, and other pages add [later fills](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/single-prints/). Those imply opposite trades, since one fades the node and the other trades through it to the next high-volume node. With both outcomes allowed and no stated deciding condition, the claim cannot fail. *Mind Over Markets* reportedly has [sections on low-volume areas](https://www.thalia.de/shop/home/artikeldetails/A1025407863), but the wording was not read and the term "LVN" should not be attributed to Dalton or Steidlmayer.

The adjacent evidence does not transfer. Osler's currency study found [bounces at 60.8% for published levels and 56.2% for arbitrary ones](https://resources.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/epr/00v06n2/0007osle.pdf). **Arbitrary levels bounced more than half the time, so raw hold rates say little; the evidence lies in the 4.6-point gap, and that gap is a bounce measure, not a net return.** The same benchmark undercuts a [vendor's 59% hold rate for volume-profile support](https://deepscope.com/support-resistance-volume-profile/), which was seen only in a search summary and reportedly lacks a random-level comparison, although the markets differ. [Chung & Bellotti](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.07410) use local price extrema, and [Huang et al.](https://scholar.nycu.edu.tw/en/publications/applying-market-profile-theory-to-analyze-financial-big-data-and-/) examine high-activity points of control in Taiwan futures, reporting no returns in the abstract. If anything, that logic of price memory maps better to high-volume nodes than to thin areas.

The setup also has a mechanical confound. A fast leg spends little volume at many prices, so thin nodes may only reflect speed, and any pullback will cross some of those prices anyway. Historical volume at a price is also not current resting liquidity, and I found no microstructure study linking the two. A valid test would pre-define the node, compare LVN prices with other prices in the same leg at matched retracement depth, choose bounce, traversal speed or net return in advance, and adjust for multiple testing. [Osler's random-level bootstrap](https://resources.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/epr/00v06n2/0007osle.pdf) provides the template, while the matched design is my suggestion.

## Conclusion

The useful distinction is between a definition and an edge. "Impulse" is a label that a trader can define objectively, and the six checks do that, but objectivity says nothing about what happens next. The schools supply vocabulary, and the research supplies only conditional, mostly off-horizon results, so no threshold here can be defended by appeal to authority or evidence. Adjusting 4 x ATR or the volume multiples toward a "correct" value has no target in this literature.

Two implications follow if you revisit the idea. Fixed thresholds across 2 to 10 candles mix unlike events, so per-instrument percentiles or length-scaled baselines would make the label coherent, though not necessarily profitable. Liquidity is the recurring moderator in the crypto papers, so any retest belongs on the most liquid perpetuals, with long and short impulses separated, because [time-series momentum is reported as concentrated in bull markets](https://acfr.aut.ac.nz/__data/assets/pdf_file/0009/918729/Time_Series_and_Cross_Sectional_Momentum_in_the_Cryptocurrency_Market_with_IA.pdf) (search summary). The LVN layer is the weakest link: it adds free parameters to a filter that already lacks validation, and it should stay an explicit hypothesis until a matched-baseline test exists.
